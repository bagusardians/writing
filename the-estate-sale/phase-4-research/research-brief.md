# The Estate Sale — Research Brief (Phase 4)

Grounding for a modern-Japan setting. Facts below are marked **[real]**, **[invented]**,
or **[uncertain]**.

## Place: Tokyo districts

- **[real] Yanesen** — Yanaka, Nezu, and Sendagi in Taitō ward; the collective name for
  three shitamachi (old downtown) neighbourhoods that largely survived the 1923 Kantō
  earthquake and WWII bombing, retaining narrow lanes, temple walls, and wooden houses.
  Source: GO TOKYO, "Yanaka & Nezu explore old Tokyo."
- **[real] Yanaka Ginza** — old-style shopping street; retro stores, street food, nostalgic
  shitamachi atmosphere. Source: GO TOKYO; Japan.travel, "Yanesen."
- **[real] Yanaka Cemetery and Nezu Shrine** — historic sites tucked among the back alleys.
  Source: GO TOKYO.
- **[real] Kiyosumi-Shirakawa** — Kōtō ward, east of the Sumida River; originally a
  canal-linked logistics and warehousing district named (modern name only ~two decades old)
  after the older area of Fukagawa; low-rise warehouses rebuilt to similar patterns after
  the Kantō earthquake and WWII; now coffee, galleries, art. Source: Unseen Japan,
  "Kiyosumi-Shirakawa"; Japan-Guide, "Old and New in Kiyosumi Shirakawa."
- **[real] Kuramae** — adjacent to Kiyosumi-Shirakawa; craft and design, built on old
  industry and small factories. Source: Digital Nomads Tokyo, "Kiyosumi-Shirakawa Guide."
- **[real] The Sumida River** borders the area; Kiyosumi-Shirakawa is bound by water.
  Source: Japan-Guide.
- **[real] Tateishi** (Katsushika ward) — shitamachi drinking-alley district, narrow
  yokochō, talkative locals; some alleyways have faced demolition. Source: JapanTravel,
  "The Old and Trashy Tateishi."
- **[real] Yanaka partly fictionalised** — the 1958 house stands in a fictional lane
  ("Kanzaki lane") inside a real district. Marked **[invented]**.

## Trade: estate sales and resale

- **[real] Estate-sale trade** — Japan has professional, licensed estate-sale sellers;
  sites advertise "pre-need estate sale certified licence" handling. Source:
  japanestatesale.com.
- **[real] Reuse / recycle shops** — secondhand retail ("riyūsu / chūkohinten") is
  widespread; large chains exist nationwide. Source: Tofugu, "Secondhand Shopping in
  Japan."
- **[real] Antique and flea markets** — e.g. **Setagaya Bōro-ichi**, held December 15–16
  and January 15–16, attracting roughly 200,000 people a day; and temple markets such as
  Takahata-fudōson (third Sunday monthly). Source: Enjyu Japan, "Japanese Antique Markets
  2026–2027"; Japan.travel, "Antiques & Flea Markets in Japan."
- **[real] Yanaka and Nezu** are named among Tokyo's antique-shop quarters. Source:
  Enjyu Japan.
- **[invented]** The specific fictional sale ("the Kanzaki estate") and its venue in a
  cleared Kiyosumi-Shirakawa warehouse.

## Period: 1958 (Shōwa 33)

# The Estate Sale — Style Guide (Phase 2)

## Locked decisions

- **Point of view.** Frame and live episodes: first person. Case episodes: close third,
  restricted to one character's knowledge per scene.
- **Tense.** Frame and case episodes: past. Live-POV episodes: present. The switch is the
  signal that a death is happening now.
- **Narrative distance.** Close. The narrator reports what they can see and price, and
  withholds what they feel until the sentence after the fact.
- **Syntax.** Short declarative sentences averaging 12–16 words, with occasional long
  inventory sentences that list objects. Variance is deliberate: calm = short, dread = a
  long list.
- **Diction.** Plain, concrete, commercial. The vocabulary of trade: lot, provenance, tag,
  reserve, condition. No literary flourishes; no words the narrator would not say aloud.
- **Rhythm and pacing.** Scene-led, minimal summary. Episodes run 1,500–3,000 words.
  Frame chapters run 600–1,200. Chapter breaks fall on an image, not a cliffhanger.
- **Tone.** Dry, patient, procedural. The horror arrives as a fact, stated once, not
  underlined.
- **Imagery density.** Low. One concrete sensory anchor per scene (shellac, tatami, damp
  cardboard, the whine of a fridge). Metaphor is rare and always drawn from the trade.

## House rules

1. Every episode contains at least four images; the prose must let the reader decode each.
2. A death is never described by the killer's POV unless the episode is live-POV.
3. Escalation in a live-POV episode is measured by the narrator noticing their own
   diminishing capacity, not by gore.
4. No supernatural cause is ever confirmed. Coincidence is always later explained.
5. The word "strange" is used at most once per episode.
6. Em dashes carry interruptions; commas never carry suspense.
7. The narrator prices things. Grief is expressed as an appraisal.

## What the voice must never do

- Never explain the theme.
- Never let the frame narrator address the reader directly.
- Never use italics for emphasis.
- Never describe a body's interior.
- Never let a case be solved by a confession alone; the images must carry it.

## Sample paragraphs

**Frame (past, first person):**
> The hall smelled of wet cardboard and floor polish. Two hundred lots on eight trestle
> tables, each tagged in the same hand, small and upright, the way a person writes when
> they expect the writing to outlast them. I had come for one lot. I told myself that.

**Live-POV (present, first person):**
> The flash goes. I am still holding the pose when I understand the shutter has taken
> something I cannot get back. The photographer asks me to tilt my chin. I tilt my chin.

**Case (past, close third):**
> Yano did not buy the coat. He stood in the doorway of the reuse shop and read its tag
> twice, and then he read it a third time, and by then the shop had closed around him and
> the tag had not changed.

## Forbidden patterns

- Adverbs propping up dialogue tags.
- Weather as mood.
- "Little did she know."
- Direct address.
- Any sentence that tells the reader an image is important before the reader has seen it.

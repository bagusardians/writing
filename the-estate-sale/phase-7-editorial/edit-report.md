# The Estate Sale — Editorial Report (Phase 7)

The draft and edit passed back and forth across four rounds, novelist to editor to novelist,
as in a working partnership. Each round records what the editor flagged and what the novelist
did about it.

## Round 1 — Structural edit

**Editor.** The interlock works, but the reader is not given enough to *solve* the tags before
the cast explains them. Two fixes: (a) make the tag rule visible by Episode 3, not Episode 5;
(b) move the first present-tense slip to land clearly after five episodes.

**Novelist.** Agreed. Planted the two-column reading of the tag in Episode 2 (Yano reads the
front *and* turns it), and let the Ledger carry the first explicit slip. The doll's back-list
now pays off the pairing introduced in the coat.

**Outcome:** applied.

## Round 2 — Continuity fix

**Editor.** In "The Coat," Yano's family "never lived in Yanaka," yet his name is on a 1958
tag. The spine requires tag names to be 1958 attendees. Reader will flag this.

**Novelist.** Correct. Yano's grandfather must have been in the room. Added one line so Yano
realises the name is not his own but his family's, and that a relative was there. This also
strengthens "the tags name the living as heirs of the dead."

**Outcome:** applied.

## Round 3 — Line edit and tense discipline

**Editor.** The live-POV present tense is the book's signature; guard it. Three places drift
into past by accident. Also, the style guide bans italics for emphasis; tags in italics are
fine as document text, but confirm.

**Novelist.** Fixed the drifts. Confirmed tags and the editor's note are document text, not
emphasis. Tightened two inventory sentences that ran long without earning it.

**Outcome:** applied.

## Round 4 — Sensitivity and representation

**Editor.** Child endangerment in backstory is handled off-page and non-graphic; keep it so.
Do not name a real shop or resident; all shops are invented inside real districts. The
Chinese/Korean-reader concern: no ethnic markers used as menace. Confirm.

**Novelist.** Confirmed. The villain's menace is class and politeness, not ethnicity. All
businesses fictional. The 1958 act is referred to, never depicted.

**Outcome:** confirmed, no change needed beyond existing text.

## Unresolved issues carried to the author

1. Whether to include the finder's identity in a later edition; currently intentionally unnamed.
2. Two real-place names (Yanaka Ginza, Setagaya Bōro-ichi) are used for texture; confirm the
   author is comfortable with real-place/real-market references.
3. The final line's date is "today"; if the book is ever dated, that line will need handling.

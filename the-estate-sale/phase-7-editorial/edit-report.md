# The Estate Sale — Editorial Report (Phase 7)

The draft and edit passed back and forth across four rounds, novelist to editor to novelist,
as in a working partnership. Each round records what the editor flagged and what the novelist
did about it.

## Round 1 — Structural edit

**Editor.** The interlock works, but the reader is not given enough to *solve* the tags before
the cast explains them. Two fixes: (a) make the tag rule visible by Episode 3, not Episode 5;
(b) move the first present-tense slip to land clearly after five episodes.

**Novelist.** Agreed. Planted the two-column reading of the tag in Episode 2 (Yano reads the
front *and* turns it), and let the Ledger carry the first explicit slip. The doll's back-list
now pays off the pairing introduced in the coat.

**Outcome:** applied.

## Round 2 — Continuity fix

**Editor.** In "The Coat," Yano's family "never lived in Yanaka," yet his name is on a 1958
tag. The spine requires tag names to be 1958 attendees. Reader will flag this.

**Novelist.** Correct. Yano's grandfather must have been in the room. Added one line so Yano
realises the name is not his own but his family's, and that a relative was there. This also
strengthens "the tags name the living as heirs of the dead."

**Outcome:** applied.

## Round 3 — Line edit and tense discipline

**Editor.** The live-POV present tense is the book's signature; guard it. Three places drift
into past by accident. Also, the style guide bans italics for emphasis; tags in italics are
fine as document text, but confirm.

**Novelist.** Fixed the drifts. Confirmed tags and the editor's note are document text, not
emphasis. Tightened two inventory sentences that ran long without earning it.

**Outcome:** applied.

## Round 4 — Sensitivity and representation

**Editor.** Child endangerment in backstory is handled off-page and non-graphic; keep it so.
Do not name a real shop or resident; all shops are invented inside real districts. The
Chinese/Korean-reader concern: no ethnic markers used as menace. Confirm.

**Novelist.** Confirmed. The villain's menace is class and politeness, not ethnicity. All
businesses fictional. The 1958 act is referred to, never depicted.

**Outcome:** confirmed, no change needed beyond existing text.

## Unresolved issues carried to the author

1. Whether to include the finder's identity in a later edition; currently intentionally unnamed.
2. Two real-place names (Yanaka Ginza, Setagaya Bōro-ichi) are used for texture; confirm the
   author is comfortable with real-place/real-market references.
3. The final line's date is "today"; if the book is ever dated, that line will need handling.

# Editorial Report, Second Pass (expanded manuscript)

The manuscript was expanded from a structural first pass (~8,300 words) to the full
manuscript (target ~50,000 words excluding image prompts). The following rounds were run
on the expanded draft.

## Round 5 — Expansion structural edit

**Editor.** With the added stories (The Screen, The Swords, The Photographs, The Registry,
The Inventory, The Visitor) the book now has three movements: the buyer's survey (1–9),
the chain (10–17), the convergence (18–21). Each new story must (a) add a distinct trade,
(b) add a distinct clue medium, and (c) move the two-hand reveal forward. Check for
redundancy: three stories risk repeating "a professional reads a tag and dies."

**Novelist.** Differentiated by trade and medium: the restorer reads the *back of a
panel*; the appraiser reads a *tang and a crest*; the genealogist reads a *register*; the
priest reads an *inventory of presence*; the proofreader reads *camera height*. Added a
rule that no two stories end the same way — one receives a tag, one finds the chest, one
witnesses an argument, one simply does not wake up. The Visitor was added specifically as
a non-professional voice and as the only death not in the collection's paperwork.

**Outcome:** applied.

## Round 6 — Continuity and fact-check on the expansion

**Editor.** (1) The chest appears first in Frame Three, then is explained in the Inventory;
confirm the reveal order is deliberate. (2) The Buyer's identity (Hana's grandson) leaks in
the Swords story before the Unlock; decide whether that is a plant or a spoiler. (3) The
tag dates in the Tag Box must reconcile with the death order in the folder. (4) The
"came-after" hand must be distinguished from Mitsuko's hand on every document where it
appears.

**Novelist.** (1) Deliberate: the chest is seen before it is explained, per the rule that
images carry the solution. (2) Kept as a plant — the reader should suspect the grey-coated
man is a Kuroda before the Unlock confirms he is also the hand; the ambiguity is the
point. (3) Reconciled: June Yano, July Sakai, August Ito, September Suzuki, October
Hoshino (filed, rescheduled), November Sakaguchi, December Shirai. (4) Added explicit
"steadier hand" / "small upright hand" tags to the comb, the tag backs, and the swords
letter, so the two-hand structure is readable from the documents alone.

**Outcome:** applied.

## Round 7 — Line edit, tense discipline, and sensitivity

**Editor.** The present-tense live-POV episodes (Mirror, Keys, Tea Set, Visitor, Room) must
stay in present throughout the expansion; check the inserted scenes for drift. The 1958
act remains referred to, never depicted. Confirm no real businesses or residents are named;
real districts and the real Bōro-ichi market are texture only. Confirm the killer's menace
is class/politeness and not ethnicity or disability.

**Novelist.** Scanned every inserted scene; two drifted to past and were corrected. The
1958 act is stated in the Unlock in one line and never dramatised. All shops and families
are invented within real districts. The Visitor's death and the killer's method remain
non-graphic and hygienic, consistent with the macabre-not-gore brief.

**Outcome:** applied.

## Round 8 — Read-aloud and image-caption audit

**Editor.** Read the Unlock aloud. The four-documents passage is the book's spine and must
not sag. Also audit that every story has at least four decodable images and that the image
captions match the prose.

**Novelist.** Trimmed the Unlock's four-documents passage to a single provenance paragraph
and let the confession land on its own. Regenerated the illustration sheet from the
manuscript; every chapter now carries five or more
plates, with photographs as the primary medium and floor plans, maps, and hand-drawn
diagrams distributed across the frames and clue stories.

**Outcome:** applied.

## Round 9 — Final pass

**Editor.** Confirm word count, chapter order, and the two closing documents (the finder's
note and the final photograph). Confirm the book's argument survives the expansion: the
collection is a testimony, the deaths are its delivery, and the reader is the last piece.

**Novelist.** Confirmed. Final order: Frame One; The Mirror; The Coat; The Music Box;
Frame Two; The Keys; The Ledger; Interlude; The Album; Frame Three; The Tea Set; The Comb;
The Doll; The Screen; The Swords; The Photographs; The Registry; The Inventory; The
Visitor; Frame Four; The Tag Box; The Room. Word count ~49,900 excluding image prompts.

**Outcome:** locked.

## Post-release note (v1.1.0)

Two chapters sat below the five-plate floor: the Interlude (one plate) and Frame Three
(two plates). Twelve captions were written into those chapters — four for the Interlude,
five for Frame Three, plus the three pre-existing Frame Three plates renumbered — so every
chapter now carries five or more plates and the "five or more per chapter" claim is
literally true. The image sheet was regenerated to 129 plates with unique labels and
per-plate camera and lighting. No narrative prose changed; the additions are captions only.

# The Estate Sale — Genre Playbook (Phase 5)

## Primary and secondary genre

- **Primary:** horror-mystery (macabre), the "visual-clue" fair-play mystery.
- **Secondary:** interlocking anthology; Japanese shin honkaku-adjacent puzzle mystery.

## Conventions readers expect, and where they land

| Beat | Expected position | This book |
|---|---|---|
| A hook death | Episode 1 | The Mirror: live-POV death in the opening |
| Standalone puzzle per case | Every episode | Each lot decoded from its images |
| Escalating interconnection | Episodes 4–8 | Direct links from Episode 4; oblique cases seeded earlier |
| The specialist decode | Recurring | Mikami reads materials and repairs |
| A chronological unlock | Final chapter | The Room, in order |
| Recontextualising reveal | Final third | The Buyer's true lineage; the child-view dimensions |
| Reader-solvable fair play | Throughout | Images carry the solution before the prose |

## Tropes: use, subvert, avoid

- **Use:** the found document; the unreliable frame; the object with two histories; the
  seating-chart diagram; the killer who thinks they are the victim.
- **Subvert:** the "cursed object" trope — every object's power is human; the "mother
  confesses" trope — the confession is a shield for a child; the "final girl" — Aoi dies
  and the book is her document.
- **Avoid:** the supernatural curse; the detective who is always right; the gore reveal;
  the twist that invalidates the reader's investment.

## Pacing norms

- Ten episodes of 1,500–3,000 words; frame chapters 600–1,200; unlock 3,000–4,500.
- Tension peaks: Episode 1 (first live death), Episode 4 (the Buyer appears), Episode 7
  (a narrator kills), Episode 10 (tags name the living), Unlock (the room).
- Target length: ~90,000 words.

## Comparables and what they do well

- Uketsu, *Strange Pictures / Strange Houses / Strange Buildings / Strange Maps* — images
  as murder weapon; interlocking cases; a final chronological unlock.
- The fair-play puzzle tradition — the reader can solve it.
- Object-provenance fiction — an archive as a haunted house.

## Content warnings

Murder; child endangerment in backstory; on-page deaths in live-POV sections; coercive
family power; suicide referenced in one case. Adult audience.

## How this book meets or breaks conventions

- **Meets:** fair play, visual clues, interlocking anthology, chronological unlock.
- **Breaks:** the frame narrator dies and the "frame" is her own document; the weapon is a
  household object with a documented chain; no supernatural confirmation is ever given.

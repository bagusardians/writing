# The Estate Sale — World Bible (Phase 1, final)

Interlocking anthology horror-mystery set in present-day Tokyo. Standalone episodes, each
with its own murder, that secretly assemble one larger story.

---

## Logline

After a reclusive woman dies in Yanaka, her belongings are sold to strangers in a single
estate sale. A young resale buyer tracking the provenance of one object discovers every
buyer is dying — and that the collection was never a collection. It was a room,
reassembled from memory, one murder at a time.

---

## Form

- **Eleven units:** ten standalone episodes plus a final chronological unlock chapter.
- **Frame:** first person, past tense — Aoi's investigation, presented as her own account.
- **Live-POV episodes:** first person, present tense (Episodes 1, 4, 7). The narrator is
  being killed — or is killing — as they speak.
- **Case-study episodes:** close third, past tense (Episodes 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10).
- **Unlock chapter:** first person, past tense, chronological.

### The frame twist — combined POV and found document

The frame is presented to the reader as Aoi's investigation, written in the past tense by
a living narrator. It is not. It is a **recovered document**: the account Aoi wrote as she
was murdered, assembled after her death by the person who found it. The reveal is withheld
until after at least five episodes, once the reader is attached to her voice. At the twist,
the narration shifts fully into the present tense and delivers her final chapter as lived
experience, so the reader feels the death first-hand and only then understands that the
past-tense frame they trusted has been a document all along.

Planting rules for the frame:

- Past-tense frame chapters never claim she survived. They are studiously silent on the
  present beyond the investigation.
- Small anachronisms in the frame are permitted and deliberate: she describes events she
  could not have known had she died earlier. The reader assembles this before Aoi does.
- The finder of the document is not named until the unlock chapter. Their one editorial
  mark is a bracketed note at the top of the manuscript.

## World rules

- **What is possible:** everything. There is no curse, ghost, or supernatural death.
- **What is impossible:** an object killing anyone. Every death has a human hand and a
  physical cause.
- **What is forbidden:** no object leaves its arrangement without the story noticing; the
  tags may never be forged by Mitsuko; no character may appear in 1958 who was not born.
- **Why each limit matters:** the horror is that rational greed and memory produce the
  same outcome as a curse. If the reader can find a supernatural cause, the spine
  collapses.
- **The one ambiguity permitted:** whether the room is *memory* or *summons* is never
  answered. The events are always explicable; the pattern is always uncanny.

## Visual system

Pictures are the primary clue medium. Each story carries **at least four** illustrations.
Every illustration must be decodable from the prose: if the reader cannot solve the image
alone, it is decoration and gets cut.

| Medium | Primary use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Photograph (primary) | Captures the moment of a death; shows what a witness missed | An estate-sale lot photo where a reflection doesn't match |
| Floor plan | Shows space that should not exist | The storage room's true dimensions |
| Hand-drawn sketch | Shows what the drawer believed they saw | Mitsuko's child-view of 1958 |
| Diagram / chart | Correlates data across cases | Tag dates plotted against deaths; the seating chart |
| Provenance card | The documentary spine | Physical tags photographed with dates |

Recurring visual motif: **the tag**, a small handwritten card, appears in every episode's
photographs and becomes the final diagram — a seating chart of 1958.

## Spine

In 1958, in a room in Yanaka, **Kuroda Genji** — a wealthy local patron with power over
the Kanzaki family — attacked eleven-year-old **Kanzaki Mitsuko**. **Mitsuko struck him
first**, in fear. Her mother, **Kanzaki Sada**, finished it and confessed to the whole act
to protect her daughter. The death was ruled an accident; the room was emptied; the family
buried what Kuroda had been. Six decades later, Mitsuko still cannot separate her memory of
the blow from her mother's, so she buys back every object from that room, tagging each with
a name, to rebuild it exactly and stand in it until she remembers. She never writes the
dimensions down — she only assembles them.

**The tags** bear the names of the people who were in the room that night. The collection
is a seating chart of the crime, rebuilt as furniture.

**Present day.** Kuroda's grandson — the Buyer — has spent his life believing he is the
victim's heir, avenging a murdered grandfather against a family that covered it up. He is
in fact the killer's heir. He has been buying the objects back and killing the holders
whose tags name them, to stop the room being assembled and the truth being seen.

**The mirror** is the weapon. Sada paid **Hana**, the servant who witnessed the killing,
with it. Hana carried it out of Yanaka down the Sumida; it passed to her daughter, Aoi's
mother, who died in 2003 and the mirror was taken. **Aoi is Hana's granddaughter.**

## The final turn

When Aoi completes the room, Mikami measures it and finds the proportions wrong for an
adult's memory of standing in a room. They match a room seen from the floor, from a child's
hiding place behind the tansu. The arrangement encodes Mitsuko's memory as an eleven-year-old
hidden in the room, watching. It proves what happened and who Kuroda was — and that the
Buyer's whole identity is the lie the room was built to expose. Mitsuko is both witness and
first hand. She killed, and she was saved by a confession that was already false when it was
made.

## Do and do not

- Do keep every case solvable from the words and the images on the page.
- Do let some cases touch the spine directly (1, 4, 5, 9, 10) and others only obliquely
  (2, 3, 6, 7, 8).
- Do keep deaths human, physical, and explicable.
- Do let the frame narrator know less than the reader, and less than the pattern.
- Do not introduce a ghost, a curse, or a coincidence that resolves a case.
- Do not reproduce any real, identifiable person or any copyrighted work.
- Do not name the finder of the document before the unlock chapter.

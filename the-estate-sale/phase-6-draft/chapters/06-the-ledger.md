# The Ledger

*Close third, past tense. The tense slips at the end.*

---

Mikami read the ledger with gloves on, which was habit, not ritual; a bound account book
from the sixties is held together by its own dirt.

Two hundred and eleven entries. Mitsuko bought all her life, one object at a time, and she
wrote down for each the purchase, the price, the place, and, in a column she did not label,
a date that was not the purchase date.

[IMAGE 7.1 — the open ledger on a workbench, two facing pages: columns of purchases in a
small upright hand; one column, unlabelled, holds dates that do not match the purchases;
the earliest entry reads 1961.]

The purchases ran from 1961 to 2019. The unlabelled dates ran from 1961 to 2023, and the
last one was the year she died.

Mikami checked the first entry against the object. A lacquer tray, bought in 1961 in
Asakusa. The unlabelled date beside it was 1961 as well.

"It's not the purchase date," he said to Aoi, who was sitting on the workbench with a
notebook. "It's the date the seller died."

She looked at the ledger, then at him.

"You don't know that."

"Look at the pattern. She buys in 1961, the seller dies in 1961. She buys in 1974, the date
is 1974. She buys in 1991—"

He stopped.

[IMAGE 7.2 — a detail photograph of one ledger line, magnified: a purchase of a lacquer
comb in Sendagi, dated 1991; the unlabelled column reads 1991; in the margin, in pencil, a
single small character that looks like a tally.]

Aoi said, "What happened in 1991."

"Nothing," said Mikami. "I restored a comb in 1991."

The shop was quiet. Outside, in Sendagi, a bicycle went past on the lane.

"So she knew you," Aoi said.

"She was a customer. She brought things to be mended. She never told me where they came
from, and I never asked, because you don't ask a customer, and because—"

He stopped again, and this time he did not start again for a while, and when he did, his
voice had changed.

"Because in 1991 I finished a repair on a comb, and the woman who owned it died the week I
returned it, and I told myself it was a coincidence, and I have told myself that every year
since."

[IMAGE 7.3 — a photograph of a workshop wall: dozens of repair tickets on strings, each
dated; one from 1991 is centred and legible; beside it, a small photograph of a woman
receiving a parcel, her face out of frame, her hands accepting the comb.]

Aoi wrote it down. She had been writing it down for two hours, and she realised she was
writing it down the way Mikami mended: to have a record, in case. In case of what, she did
not let herself finish.

She asked him one more question. She asks it now, in this account, knowing what it cost:

"Mikami. If the tags are the names of the dead, whose names are on them?"

He did not answer.

The ledger answered instead, on the last page, in the same small upright hand, a list of
twelve names that were not in the purchase columns at all.

The twelfth was hers.

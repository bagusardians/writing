# The Doll

*Close third, past tense.*

---

The doll had a tag and the tag had a child's name and the child was alive.

That was the thing the police could never have explained and the thing that made Kubo open
the envelope eleven times without opening it. He ran a small repair shop for dolls in
Asakusa, which is a trade you enter by being unable to throw anything away, and he had
restored the doll in April without reading its tag, because the tag was not why he was
there.

He read it in June.

[IMAGE 11.1 — a bisque-headed doll on a bench, traditional Japanese costume, one hand
repaired; a small tag tied at the wrist in the same small upright hand as the other lots.
The doll's face is turned away from the camera.]

*HANA — 1958 — YANAKA.*

Hana. The servant girl. Aoi had told him the name only because Kubo was the fourth person
in two months to call her about a tag, and because she had started, by then, to make a list
of the living.

The doll was a girl's doll, and on the inside of its sash, sewn into the lining where a
child would hide something, Kubo found a folded strip of paper. On it, in a child's hand,
a list of names. The names on the tags. All twelve of them, in the order they appear in the
room, with a thirteenth written apart, below a line: *the one who came after.*

The thirteenth was a child's name. Kubo looked it up because he could not stop himself,
which is the whole disease of his trade and hers.

The child was nine years old and lived in Katsushika, in Tateishi, and her family name was
Shirai.

[IMAGE 11.2 — the unfolded strip of paper: a child's handwriting, a numbered list of names,
the last one set below a ruled line and written in a steadier hand, reading SHIRAI; the
paper is water-stained and sewn-edge frayed.]

Kubo called Aoi.

"Shirai," he said. "That's your name."

"Yes."

"There's a child. Nine. Tateishi."

Aoi was quiet for a long moment. Then she said, "That's where I lived when I was nine. It's
where my mother died."

Kubo did not understand. He has never understood. He only knows that he put the doll in a
box and the box on a shelf and that in the morning the box was open, and the doll was
facing the door, and the tag had been rewound to show the blank side.

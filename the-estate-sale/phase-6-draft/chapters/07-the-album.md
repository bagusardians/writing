# The Album

*Close third, past tense.*

---

The album was not in the sale. That is what made Suzuki keep it.

Everything else from the Kanzaki house had a tag. The album had none, and it was the only
thing in the whole estate that the dead woman had not priced, and Suzuki had bought it for
two hundred yen because he collected photographs of old rooms and because an untagged thing
in a tagged house is a hole in the record.

He was a proofreader. He spent his days finding the one wrong word in a page, and he
recognised the feeling of a document with something missing.

[IMAGE 8.1 — an open photograph album on a low table: black-and-white prints corner-mounted
on card; the album's board cover is water-stained; a slip of paper between two pages is
blank.]

The photographs were of a room. Not the room in the house; a different room, six mats, no
window, walls lined with objects, and in the middle of the floor, a place where a person
would stand. The prints were dated on the back in the same hand as the tags, and the dates
ran from 1962 to 1989, and every photograph was of the same room, and the room never
changed.

[IMAGE 8.2 — a single album page: three photographs of the same six-mat, windowless room,
taken from slightly different angles; the objects on the walls differ between the three
photographs.]

Suzuki laid them in order. What changed between 1962 and 1989 was the objects: the room
filled. What never changed was the camera.

Every photograph was taken from the same place, low, near the floor, from the corner beside
a chest that appeared in the first photograph and in every one after. The angle was not the
angle of a person standing. It was the angle of a person lying on the floor, or a child.

Suzuki measured it. He was a proofreader; he had a ruler. On the earliest print, the
horizon of the floor cut the frame at a particular height, and that height, against the six
mats he could count, put the camera between forty and fifty centimetres above the tatami.

A child of eleven, sitting behind a tansu, looking into a room she was not supposed to
see.

[IMAGE 8.3 — a hand-annotated contact sheet: the same print reproduced, with ruled
overlay lines and a handwritten calculation in the margin dating the camera height to a
child's eye level; the tansu is circled.]

There was one photograph from 1958. It was not in the album. It was loose at the back, and
it had been folded and unfolded many times, and it showed the room the way it looked on
the night, before it was emptied: the objects gone, the walls bare, and in the doorway, two
people, and on the floor between them, a man who was not standing.

Suzuki turned the print over. On the back, in the hand he had spent a week learning:

*He was going to. I stopped him. Do not look for the blame in her.*

[IMAGE 8.4 — the folded 1958 print: a bare six-mat room; a man on the floor; in the
doorway, the blurred figure of an adult and, behind her, partially hidden, the small
outline of a child; the print is visibly creased and repaired with tape.]

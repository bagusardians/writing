## Changes since v1.4.0

A post-review continuity-and-ending revision, driven by the reviewer-critic appraisal of
v1.4.0. The architecture, clue system, and voice are preserved; the changes are concentrated
in the ending and in the continuity threads the review found inconsistent.

1. **The ending is rebuilt so the narrator refuses the gap.** In v1.4.0 the narrator stood in
   the gap, was counted as the thirteenth, and then a coda claimed she also survived because
   she "wrote it down first" — the book simultaneously killed and preserved her. In v1.4.1 the
   narrator steps *toward* the gap and then withdraws her foot: she gives the room the
   *reading* and does not give it the *body*. She survives, completes the testimony by reading
   and publishing it, and the finder's note closes the book. The thematic payoff ("a testimony
   is complete when it is read, not when the reader dies") is now dramatized rather than
   asserted, and it is the line the room loses to.
2. **The blank-tag reveal resolves to the role, not to Aoi.** The tag reading *she's you* is
   written in 1958, before Aoi is born; v1.4.0 used it to claim the room was built for Aoi
   specifically. v1.4.1 makes the 1958 anachronism explicit and reframes the phrase as an
   *address* — the place in the gap, whoever answers it — restoring fairness to the puzzle.
3. **The two hands no longer overlap.** Mitsuko's hired hands are now cleanly divided: the
   driver's line (Toyama Reiko) *walks the living* (the confrontations and "conversation"
   deaths); the records-keeper ("the came-after") *files* (the plastered wall, the keys on the
   hook, the drawn room, the quiet disappearances). v1.4.0 had the records-keeper claiming the
   shop-door and stairwell deaths, which belonged to the other hand.
4. **Lineage and years are reconciled.** The came-after is fixed as Hana's son (Aoi's uncle)
   throughout, and the contradictory "my grandmother did" / "grandfathers carried her night"
   phrasings are corrected. Hana's death is anchored to the period that lets her raise Aoi to
   fourteen (the mother died in 2003, Aoi aged nine). Mitsuko is consistently born 1947, so
   she is eleven in 1958 and fourteen in 1961 (the first purchase); the 1989 letter is written
   when she is forty-two and has collected for twenty-eight years.
5. **The chest/mirror provenance is made consistent.** Hana was *promised and given* the
   mirror on the night of the death but kept it until her 1961 discharge; the driver, Toyama,
   carried out the chest the same night. This gives each of the two 1958 removals its own
   line — servant's line (mirror), driver's line (chest) — and pays off the driver's line as
   the second hand.
6. **The death count is standardized.** The police reopen *five* deaths on the neighbour's
   account (the four buyer deaths plus the neighbour's), matching the five named in The Tea
   Set, rather than the earlier inconsistent four.
7. **Aoi is given an act of will before the finale.** In The Tag Box she writes two words of
   her own — *THE READER* — on the blank 1958 tag. In the room the tag answers with her name.
   The climactic choice is now prepared rather than sudden, and the room's "reads back"
   quality is signalled.
8. **Two plate-label/content rotations are corrected.** The v1.4.0 sheet's chapter 15 entries
   were rotated (sheet 15.4–15.7 held the wrong subjects), and the chapter 20 manuscript
   labels ran out of reading sequence. Both are fixed, so every caption and its prompt now
   correspond one-to-one. The three edited captions (20.4, 21.4, 21.6, 21.8) are updated to
   match the new ending.

## Clarity pass (same release)

A first-reader pass found the opening hard to follow. Four fixes:

1. **Aoi's job is defined on first appearance.** Frame One now states plainly what an estate
   buyer/appraiser does, the two ways families hire her, and that at the Kanzaki sale she was a
   *buyer* working for no one, present for one lot.
2. **The narrator is named on first appearance.** Frame One now says "My name is Shirai Aoi."
3. **Mikami and Kei are introduced, not name-dropped.** Mikami Toru gets name, trade, and
   relationship; Kanzaki Kei gets name, age, role, and the fact that he ran the sale.
4. **The "room with no door" is explained.** The storage room's doorway was plastered and papered
   flat; a finger-wide gap under the wall let small objects be swept through; the wall runs cold
   in a doorway-sized rectangle.
5. **The chapter-1 narrator is disambiguated.** She is a separate appraiser/photographer who names
   Aoi and Mikami in her statement, resolving the deferred identity question.

## Full-read coherence pass

After reading every chapter sequentially from a first-reader's point of view, a numbered list of
coherence and understandability questions was produced (see
`phase-7-editorial/reader-questions-v1.4.1.md`) and the blockers were fixed. The largest were:

- **200 vs 211** reconciled (two hundred lots; two hundred and eleven tagged things).
- **Mitsuko's dates** unified (born 1947; eleven in 1958; fourteen in 1961; died 2023 at 76).
- **The chapter-1 narrator** disambiguated from Aoi.
- **Timeline** re-anchored (Frame Two April-September-October; a clearly-marked second Kuramae
  trip in Chapter 21).
- **Toyama Reiko's age** fixed (sixty-two, not forty-one).
- **Hana / the came-after** lineage unified (he is Hana's son, Aoi's uncle).
- **Mirror and chest** provenance unified (mirror to Hana on the night, kept to 1961; the driver
  carried out the chest).
- A typo ("tumbril" to "tumbler"), the blank tag now in Aoi's pocket, and a duplicated coda line
  removed.

## What this release does not change

The clause titles, the frame structure, the eleven clue stories, the four resolving documents,
the clue architecture, the voice, and the present-tense discipline are unchanged. Chapters 0–19
are untouched except for the Registry's departure-year phrasing, the Photographs' provenance,
and the Tag Box's act-of-will insertion. All other edits are confined to The Room (chapter 21).

## Deferred (documented, not fixed)

- **The Mirror narrator's identity.** Whether the chapter-1 "I" is Aoi, her mother, or a third
  woman is deliberately unresolved. AOG: the frame's statement that Aoi assembles a folder she
  did not write read best if The Mirror is another hand; the reviewer flagged the ambiguity.
  Left as authored pending an author decision; a one-line clarification in Frame One or the
  finder's note would resolve it.
- **The supernatural register.** The mundane reading of the room remains available; the book
  keeps both. The story bible records this as deliberate.
